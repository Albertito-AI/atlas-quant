# Atlas Quant: research you can inspect

Financial analysis is more useful when another person can understand how it was produced. Atlas Quant helps learners and research teams examine the assumptions behind an investment simulation, reproduce a calculation, and discuss what its results can and cannot establish.

The current release is an offline, open-source research toolkit. It can compare portfolio strategies using local price data, model transaction costs, calculate risk measures and option values, and produce an HTML report with an accompanying evidence record. Its research council uses explicit rules. It does not call an artificial intelligence model or place trades.

## Workshop: Reviewing AI-Assisted Financial Research

A practical three-hour workshop for educators, analysts, risk and control teams, and professionals who need to examine financial research. Participants learn to question generated explanations, identify weaknesses in an illustrative backtest, and produce a concise record of their findings.

The session uses synthetic data and includes a guided demonstration of Atlas Quant. No programming experience is required to follow the discussion. Participants who wish to run the toolkit themselves will need a suitable Python environment; installation can be completed before the workshop or followed as a demonstration.

**Format:** Online, up to 20 participants; a tailored in-person session can be discussed separately.

## Team pilot: A reproducible research report

A fixed-scope engagement for a small team that wants to explore a more transparent research workflow. We will agree one example, set up Atlas Quant on the team's own machine, produce one evidence record and HTML report, and walk through the assumptions, limitations and steps required to reproduce the output.

**Indicative pilot fee:** S$2,500 for one agreed workflow, one report and one remote training session, subject to a short written scope and the client's ability to provide an appropriately licensed, non-sensitive example dataset. Synthetic data can be used instead.

The pilot concerns the use and interpretation of research software. It does not include individual investment advice, a certification, an independent financial-model audit, implementation of live trading, hosted infrastructure, continuous monitoring or verification of the accuracy of a third-party dataset.
